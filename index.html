Thousands Wouldn’t, But I Believe You

Premenstrual Dysphoric Disorder. It’s not bad PMS, although you’ve probably spent years letting people tell you that’s all it is, while smiling that yeah, they’re probably right.

It feels like you’re being possessed by someone who hates you and the intruder has access to every fear you’ve ever had, with no interest in keeping them quiet.

You know exactly when it’s coming. You’d think that would help, but it doesn’t. You’ve spent years doing legwork in the good weeks, banking enough goodwill to survive the bad ones. Just as you start to feel like yourself again, the clock resets. The relief when it breaks is real, but it’s brief, because the second you surface, you’re already counting down again.

This project began because that experience deserves more than a shrug and a leaflet about contraception and healthy eating. PMDD isn’t a footnote and the women living through it aren’t imagining the scale of it. We want to understand whether women are truly being heard by anyone at all, and if they’re being given real choices before life-changing decisions are made.

If you’ve lived any part of this, your experience matters - not as a data point, but as a piece of a picture that’s long overdue. Filling in a form takes a few minutes. What you share might help the next woman feel less alone, and less like something she has to endure quietly.

Thank you for trusting us with your story.
