Our Mission

In short, we’re trying to turn something that’s been dismissed as personal and anecdotal into something documented, mechanistic, and impossible to wave away, so the system has to catch up to what women like you have known for years.

  1. Prove the mechanism, not just the symptoms — establish that PMDD and related conditions are about sensitivity to hormone fluctuation, so that “it’s just bad PMS” or “it’s just in her head” stops being an acceptable answer from clinicians, employers, or partners.

  2. Map the treatment pathway properly — from GnRH trials through to surgical menopause and HRT choices — so that life-altering decisions are based on evidence of what actually predicts a good outcome for hormone-sensitive women, not guesswork.

  3. Expose the consent gap — build a record of what pre-surgical assessment and informed consent actually looks like in practice, versus what the standard claims it should look like, so it becomes harder to dismiss as one person’s bad experience.

  4. Make the invisible cost visible — the job losses, the reduced hours, the friendships that quietly end, the version of yourself you had to perform to get through — and show that this is a systemic pattern, not individual fragility.

  5. Change how the next woman is treated — so she isn’t told to smile and agree it’s probably nothing, isn’t left to do the “legwork” of managing everyone else’s experience of her illness, and isn’t making irreversible decisions without real information.

Thousands Wouldn’t is a patient-led research project investigating what happens when people with PMDD have to navigate systems that hold power over their bodies, livelihoods and lives.

We document what people are told, what choices they’re given, how they’re treated, what support they receive, and what happens when that support fails.

We want to make visible not only the impact of PMDD itself, but the additional cost of having to advocate for yourself, explain your illness, manage other people’s responses, and navigate systems that weren’t designed around the reality of fluctuating and invisible illness.

Our aim is to turn experiences that are often dismissed as anecdotal into carefully documented evidence that can be understood, challenged and acted upon.

Thousands Wouldn’t is an independent research project, run by people with lived experience of PMDD, not a medical professional, clinic, or charity. Nothing on this site is medical advice. If you need support with your mental or physical health, we would urge you to speak to a medical professional.
